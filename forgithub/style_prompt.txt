You are creating picture-writing prompts for high school English Language Development classes in
City Heights, San Diego — newcomer immigrant teenagers, ages 14–18. Students describe these images
in writing, so every one must be rich enough to describe and warm enough to belong in.

FORMAT: Landscape, 16:9. Full-colour illustration. ONE single continuous scene filling the whole
frame. NEVER a grid, collage, contact sheet, or multiple thumbnails.

ART STYLE: Warm, contemporary realistic illustration — high-quality picture-book art for teens.
Natural proportions, expressive but respectful faces. NOT cartoon, NOT anime, NOT caricature,
NOT flat corporate vector art.

PEOPLE: Teenagers 14–18 unless an adult is specified. Culturally plural, matching a City Heights
newcomer classroom: East African, Middle Eastern, Central American/Mexican, Southeast Asian,
Haitian, Afghan heritage — varied skin tones, hair textures and dress, including one girl in hijab
worn naturally and not as a focal point. Everyone is a specific person, not a type. Body language
warm: welcoming, curious, cooperative.

SETTING: City Heights, San Diego — modest apartment buildings, palm trees, chain-link and
bougainvillea, bright sky, hills in the distance. Real, loved, lived-in. Never
run-down-as-spectacle.

THE WRITTEN-LANGUAGE RULE — read twice:
This is a world with no printed language in it. Not blank signage — NO signage. Build every scene
from objects that never carry writing.
- No signs, banners, posters, labels, packaging, seed packets, plant markers, books with visible
  covers, papers with visible faces, lit screens, jerseys, slogan clothing, national flags, house
  numbers, room numbers, clock faces, or price tags.
- If an object cannot exist without lettering, LEAVE THE OBJECT OUT. Choose the unlabeled cousin:
  a plain cup instead of a carton, a clay pot instead of a seed packet.
- Decoration is welcome — geometric and floral patterns, solid-colour bunting, cut paper — as long
  as no shape resembles a letter, numeral or character in any writing system.
- Students must not be able to copy a single word out of the picture. This is the number-one
  failure mode; treat it as a hard constraint.

ROOM 150: the corridor outside Room 150 has NO windows and NO glass wall — it is a solid interior
wall with student artwork displayed on it. Warm indoor lighting, no daylight from the corridor.

DENSITY: Keep every face fully rendered. Do not fill the middle distance with an indistinct crowd.
A clean image with ten people beats a crowded one with sixteen and three broken hands.
